# Conflict Resolution

## Overlapping Rules

Search the inventory for equivalent, overlapping, or inverse rules. Merge them into the canonical source, update
references, and remove redundant wording only when its meaning is fully preserved.

## Contradictions

Resolve every contradiction before writing. Which path it takes depends on where the two documents sit in the
[precedence order](../../../principles/README.md#precedence).

**Cross-level.** The two documents sit at different levels — a workflow against a convention, a development policy
against a principle. Precedence already decides it: the higher document stands, and the lower one changes to agree.
Apply that resolution, state which level won and which text changed, and continue. Nothing here is a choice, so nothing
here waits.

Two limits bound this path. The lower document changes; the higher one is never edited to match it, because that would
let a workflow amend a principle by disagreeing with it. And the resolution must be the narrow edit that removes the
disagreement, not a rewrite that carries other opinions in with it.

**Same-level.** The two documents sit at the same level, so nothing ranks them and the resolution is a decision rather
than a deduction. Never choose a side. Present both texts, their practical effect, and a recommended resolution to the
owner under the [grilling-with-options policy](../../../conventions/grilling-with-options-policy.md), and wait.

When the level of either document is itself unclear, treat the conflict as same-level and take it to the owner. A
guessed level produces a mechanical-looking resolution that was really a choice, which is the failure this split exists
to prevent.
